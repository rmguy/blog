Macroeconomic predictions for December 10

It feels like a few trillion dollars were wiped out over a rounding error. 

Here is the timeline of the bloodbath: Nvidia reported earnings, and they were strong. But immediately after a delayed jobs report surfaced showing 120k new jobs instead of the expected 50k. Was this the primary catalyst for the sell-off? I can't ascertain that yet. 

What I _have_ taken upon myself to ascertain is whether there will be a rate cut on December 10. I believe this single decision will determin how we close the year. 

To understand why, we have to look at printing money.

Let's simplify the economic cycle to one single 10 buck transaction. 

1. I start a bank. I have 10 bucks. The government says I must buy 1 bond. 
2. I go to the treasury (the government's banker) and buy a 10 buck bond. The treasury gives this note to the fed (the money printer). 
3. The fed updates its db and tells the treasury it can use this money to eg, finance ai companies. 
4. At this point, i have what it takes to legally run a bank. What i don't have is customers.
5. My first customer arrives. He asks for a loan of 10. I have 10. BUT my fed db has 0. My "reserve" is 0.  
6. I issue him the loan anyway. The government doesn't say i can't. 
7. Now he takes this 10 and pays someone else. That other person places the 10 in their bank. 
8. I now need to settle the 10 with that bank, but I _can't_ give him my money. Only the fed can. And my fed reserve is 0. 
9. So I take a loan from this sister bank. I tell her: update your books as though I'd given you 10, and I'll give you interest plus the 10, when I have it. 
10. For my own economics to work out, this interest needs to factor into the interest my customer would have given me. 
11. Now at this point if the fed had bought my 10 buck bond from me, I would not have had to borrow. I could have given the loan from my reserve. This is Quantative Easing. The fed just created money to buy my bond.
12. Similarly if the fed, at some later date, sells me the bond back (i.e. they delete 10 from my fed db, aka my reserve) I can no longer give new loans. This is Quantative Tightening. 

So, stopping QT allows me to take on more loans. A rate cut allows me to refinance existing loans. Both together, allows me to give out a bunch of new attractive loans, a portion of which would be used to hire new people.

Why is this bad? because it also gives people money. And this risks inflation, which is already above the target 2%. 

And that's the tightrope. Improve employment, or reduce inflation? 

In this case, the tie breaker is politics. The republicans are losing states (NY and NJ) to the affordability crisis - the tariffs are making life more expensive for the common man. Giving them more money through this circuoitous macro economic channel could ease this tension. 

To summarize the current mess: 

1. Tariffs have increased costs. We are calling this the "affordability crisis."
2. The Paradox: To fix affordability, say the fed cuts rates to put money in people’s pockets (cheaper mortgage/loans).
3. The Trap: Cutting rates stimulates the economy. Tariffs raise prices. If you do both while the jobs report is strong, you pour gasoline on inflation.
4. The Wildcard: The government shutdown temporarily pulled roughly $50B out of the market, creating an artificial dip.

The Fed announced the end of QT in October. That implies they want more loans flowing. 
If they also cut rates in December, they are betting that the "affordability crisis" (high costs) is worse than the risk of inflation.

But it is a dangerous gamble. High tariffs plus cheap money usually equals skyrocketing prices. The Fed minutes from late October were conflicted on this exact point.

One thing is for certain: If the math is wrong and there’s blood on the street, somebody is going to pay.
